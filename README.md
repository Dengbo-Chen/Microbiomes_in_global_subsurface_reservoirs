Microbial Biogeography and Ecological Driving Forces Across Global Deep Subsurface Reservoirs

Abstract
Deep subsurface reservoir, one of Earth’s largest yet least explored microbial ecosystems, harbors diverse microbiomes controlling long-term biogeochemical cycling within the Earth. However, their distribution pattern, community assembly and associated metabolic strategies remain undetermined at the global scale. By analyzing 253 metagenomes across four major reservoir types (i.e., deep aquifers, salt and mine caverns, oil and gas reservoirs, and shale ecosystems), we assemble a catalogue encompassing 6,840 bacterial and archaeal metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs), revealing diverse and novel microbes inhabiting these reservoirs. These microbiomes are primarily determined by reservoir types in addition to burial depth, following with a Poisson-lognormal diversity distribution. Stochastic processes, particularly dispersal limitation and drift, emerge as dominant ecological drivers in all reservoir community assembly, underscoring the importance of historical contingency and spatial isolation. Metabolic reconstruction reveals striking functional differentiation among reservoirs, while hydrogen appears to serve as a universal energy currency. Oil and gas reservoirs form large, highly connected metabolic exchange networks, whereas the other reservoirs display smaller, more modular networks. This study establishes a genome-resolved framework linking microbial biogeography, ecological assembly and metabolic strategies to geological constraints, advancing the comprehensive understanding of microbial persistence in Earth’s most inaccessible ecosystems.
Keywords 
Deep subsurface, microbial biogeography, metagenomics, reservoir ecosystems, community assembly

## Data

The study integrates:
- 253 publicly available metagenomes (JGI, NCBI SRA, CNCB)
- 12 newly generated deep subsurface samples from China
